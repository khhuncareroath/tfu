Self-study guide

1. Two workspaces

Physical notebookMessy brainstorms, daily micro-lecture notes, friction logs, hand-sketched customer workflows. Focus on synthesis, not neatness.
Computer archiveThis app: curriculum PDFs, weekly lecture summaries, user interviews, assignments.
Professor’s ruleDon’t speed-read modules. Read the PDF, complete the daily notebook exercises, test hypotheses in the field, and submit findings for evaluation.

2. Rhythm

Every weekReview your pinned curriculum at the start of the week.
Every dayCheck in with your professor for a micro-lecture, discuss concepts, and refine your startup hypothesis.Consistency replaces intensity.

3. Monthly founder evaluation

At the end of each four-week block, submit notebook logs and fieldwork data. Each pillar is graded 1–10.

Problem RigorCustomer empathy, no false validation, focus on severe friction.9–10: verified “bleeding neck” pain backed by raw user quotes.
ExecutionWeekly schedules, notebook diligence, task completion.9–10: 100% of weekly fieldwork and structured note logs.
Business SynthesisBusiness theory (unit economics, monetization) joined with product architecture.9–10: flawless translation of customer needs into viable models.
ResilienceAdapting when hypotheses fail or interviews disprove assumptions.9–10: treats negative feedback as data and pivots without ego.

Grading scale

9–10 Elite Founder TrackExceptional rigor, flawless execution, venture-grade insights.
7–8 Solid ExecutionGood understanding, meets all core milestones consistently.
Below 6 Needs RevisionSurface-level analysis, guessing without customer proof.
